Governance: your money, your choice

How changes to Landing Pad will be decided, so that every person has an equal say and no vote can harm anyone who did not agree to it.

Planned for the real launch on mainnet. Not active during the public test.During the test, the only vote is the current rule vote on three proposals. Everything on this page starts after mainnet.

One rule above all others: no change is ever forced on anyone's money. Every update ships as a new version of the contracts. Coins that already exist keep their rules forever, including their reserve and their floor. Anyone who prefers the new version can move to it. Anyone who doesn't, stays. A holder with $10 and a holder with $10 million have exactly the same right: each decides alone what happens to their own money.

What no vote can ever change

  • The floor and the reserve of any coin. No vote can touch them, lower them or move them.
  • The launchpad's share is fixed at 2%. No vote can raise or lower it. Votes can only change how the rest of the fee is split between the coin's creator and the coin's own reserve.
  • The reserve's share can never fall below 2%. That share is what lifts the floor for every holder, so a vote can raise it but never cut it below today's level.
  • A hard cap on fees. The total fee on a trade can never be set above 7%: the fixed 2% for the launchpad plus at most 5% for the creator and the reserve together.
  • No treasury to vote away. There is no pool of shared money that a vote could send somewhere, which removes the main reason to attack a governance system.
  • No special powers for the team. The team votes with its own coins like anyone else.

The brake on mass selling

A brake on mass selling is one of the proposals in the current community vote. If holders approve it, these limits apply to it, and no later vote can break them:

  • It only touches the part of a sale above the floor. Selling back at the floor price always pays the normal fee, with no brake, ever.
  • All of the extra goes to the coin's reserve. The launchpad keeps its fixed 2% and the creator keeps their normal share; neither earns anything from the brake. Every cent of it lifts the floor for the holders who stay.
  • It has a hard ceiling. The fee on selling above the floor can rise from the normal level to at most 25%, and only while the price is falling from its recent high. It eases back as the market calms.
  • Votes can tune it, within those limits. The two houses can change how fast the brake climbs, or switch it off, but they cannot lift the 25% ceiling, apply it at the floor, or send any of it anywhere but the reserve.

Because none of the brake goes to the launchpad or the creator, it sits outside the 7% cap on normal fees. You can try it with your own numbers in the simulator.

Shared decisions need two houses to agree

Some decisions affect everyone going forward, such as the creator and reserve shares of the fee on new launches (today 1% and 2%), or adding a network. These need a two-thirds majority in both houses. Neither house can overrule the other.

Coin house

One $LPAD = one vote. It represents the people who carry the financial risk. One-coin-one-vote is the only weighting that splitting coins across many wallets cannot game.

People house

One verified person = one vote, whatever they hold. It protects small holders from being outvoted by large ones. Voters prove they are one unique human through a privacy-preserving check that does not reveal who they are.

So large holders cannot impose a change on everyone else, and a crowd cannot impose a change on the people whose money is at stake.

How a decision is made

  1. Public discussion, 7 days. The proposal is posted in full, with its effect on fees and holders spelled out.
  2. Vote, 7 days, in both houses. Balances are counted at the moment the proposal is published, so buying coins after the announcement adds no votes. Votes are cast by signing with a wallet, cost nothing, and are public for anyone to check.
  3. Waiting period, 14 days. Nothing happens until it ends, so everyone can review the result.
  4. Release as an opt-in version. The change applies to new launches and to anyone who chooses to move. Nobody else is affected.

What $LPAD will be used for, after mainnet

  • Launching stays free. Creating a coin costs nothing beyond the network fee, as on other launchpads.
  • Optional promotion, paid into a burn. Creators who want more visibility on landingpad.fun, such as a trending spot, a featured banner or a verified-creator badge, can pay in SOL or USDC. The contract uses every payment to buy $LPAD on the market and burn it. Nobody has to pay to launch or to trade.
  • Voting. $LPAD is the coin house in the two-house system above.
  • Rewards for testers and bug finders. Useful feedback and confirmed bugs are rewarded in $LPAD from the launchpad's own holdings.
  • A deposit to propose. Anyone submitting a proposal deposits $LPAD. It is returned if the proposal reaches minimum turnout and burned if it is spam.

None of this touches the launchpad's fixed 2% share, and none of it promises holders any income. $LPAD remains a meme coin with no floor.

Why this design

We studied what went wrong elsewhere before writing this:

  • Whale capture. In token-weighted systems a few wallets often hold most of the voting power, and groups have bought tokens on the open market to push through proposals that most of the community opposed. Here, a coin-house win alone is never enough, and balances are counted before a proposal is announced.
  • Wallet splitting. Systems that try to soften whale power by weighting small wallets more can be undone by splitting one balance across many wallets. That is why the coin house uses one-coin-one-vote and fairness for small holders comes from the people house instead.
  • The right to leave. The strongest protection in any governance system is being able to walk away before a change affects you. On Landing Pad that right already exists: every holder can sell back to the reserve at the floor, any time, and no vote can change their coin's rules.

Honest limits

  • Most token holders in most projects don't vote. If turnout is low, proposals will not pass, and that is the safe failure.
  • Proving you are one person is not perfect. Verification will be optional; anyone who prefers not to verify still votes in the coin house.
  • Until $LPAD is widely held, a few wallets could carry the coin house. That is why thresholds will be set from real participation before the system becomes binding.
