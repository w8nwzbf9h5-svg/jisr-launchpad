Long (120 words)

Most meme coins crash within days, because early wallets and creator bags sell into a pool that only exists while people keep buying. Landing Pad changes the rules. Each coin has a supply of 1 billion with no presale and no team allocation. The first 800 million coins sell at one fixed price, $0.0001, and the full value of every coin sold goes into that coin's reserve, which has no withdraw. Any holder can sell back to the reserve at the floor price at any time. A 5% fee on every trade pays 1% to the creator, 2% to the launchpad and 2% into the reserve, so the floor rises with trading and is never lowered. Landing Pad is in public test on Solana devnet and Ethereum Sepolia, with unaudited contracts and test money.

Press release

Landing Pad opens public test of a meme coin launchpad with a floor paid for in advance

Dubai, 25 November 2026. Landing Pad today opened a public test of a meme coin launchpad built around a simple rule: the money behind a coin is collected before the coins go out, and it can only leave by buying coins back.

On most launchpads, a coin's price depends on the next buyer. When early wallets sell, the price slides back down the curve, and late buyers are often left with coins nobody will buy. Landing Pad sells the first 800 million coins of every launch at one fixed price and places the full value of every coin sold in a reserve that no person controls. Any holder can sell back to that reserve at the floor price, at any time.

Every trade pays a 5% fee: 1% to the coin's creator, 2% to the launchpad and 2% to the coin's own reserve. Because fees add money to the reserve without creating new coins, a coin's floor rises as it trades and is never lowered. Creators receive no coins at launch, so their income depends on a coin staying active rather than on selling a bag.

"We wanted the worst case to be something you can read before you buy," the Landing Pad team said. "Put in $1,000 and sell straight back, and you get $902.50: the floor, less the fee each way. A floor limits the loss. It does not make a coin a good buy, and we say that on every page."

The site includes a stress test that runs in the browser: 60 wallets trading and then selling at once, or a single attacker splitting a position across 40 wallets. After every trade it checks that the reserve can still buy back every coin at the floor. The code and tests are public on GitHub, and the project has opened a community vote on three proposed rule changes.

The public test runs on Solana devnet and Ethereum Sepolia with test money that has no value. The contracts have not had an independent audit. Mainnet launch is planned once testers send 100 pieces of feedback, or when LPAD, the project's meme coin on pump.fun, reaches a $1 million market cap. LPAD has no floor and no claim on the launchpad.
