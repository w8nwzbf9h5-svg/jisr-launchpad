The 5% fee: where it goes and why 2% stays in the coin

Every trade on Landing Pad pays 5%. One percent goes to the creator, 2% to the launchpad and 2% stays in the coin's reserve, lifting its floor. Here is why it is split that way.

Every buy and every sell on Landing Pad pays a 5% fee. That is higher than some launchpads, so it is fair to ask what you get for it. The short answer: part of every fee goes back into the coin you are trading, and it raises the price you can always sell at.

The split

The fee is divided the same way on every trade, by the contract, with no one claiming it by hand:

  • 1% to the coin's creator.
  • 2% to the launchpad.
  • 2% to the coin's own reserve.

On a $1,000 buy, the fee is $50: $10 to the creator, $20 to the launchpad and $20 to the reserve. The other $950 buys coins.

The 2% that stays

The reserve is the money behind a coin's floor. It is filled by the sale itself: every coin sold puts its full price into the reserve, and no person can withdraw from it. The floor is what the reserve pays per coin when a holder sells back.

When 2% of a trade goes into the reserve, money is added without any new coins being created. The same number of coins now has more money behind it, so the floor goes up. It is never lowered.

This has a useful effect. Trading volume, which on most launchpads only rewards the platform and the fastest traders, here also strengthens the coin for every holder. A coin that people trade a lot builds a higher floor over time, even if the price on the chart moves up and down.

The 1% to the creator

On Landing Pad, the creator of a coin gets no coins at launch. There is no dev bag, no team allocation and no free share of the supply.

Instead, the creator earns 1% of every trade. That changes the creator's incentives. A creator holding 10% of the supply makes the most money by selling it at the top. A creator paid from trading fees makes money only when people keep trading. Their interest is in a coin that stays alive, not one that spikes once.

The 2% to the launchpad

The launchpad's share pays for running the platform: the site, the contracts, the work on new chains, and eventually the audit. It goes to the platform fee wallet. That is the only wallet the platform team controls; the reserves and the treasury are locked by the contracts.

The cost of a round trip

Because the fee is paid both ways, buying and selling straight back costs 9.75% in total. If you put in $1,000 at the floor and sell immediately, you get back $902.50:

  • $950 buys 9.5M coins after the 5% fee.
  • Selling the 9.5M coins back at the floor returns $950.
  • The 5% fee on the way out is $47.50.

The page shows this before you buy. It is the cost of a guaranteed exit: whatever happens to the chart, the reserve will pay the floor.

Why not a lower fee?

A lower fee would make short trades cheaper. It would also make the floor rise more slowly, pay creators less for keeping a coin alive, and make it cheaper to attack a coin with many quick trades.

Our stress test on landingpad.fun shows the trade-off. When one attacker splits a position across 40 wallets and trades in and out, the fees go to the creator, the launchpad and the reserve. The attacker loses money, and the coin's floor ends higher than it started.

The fee level is not fixed forever. Proposals to change the rules are put to a community vote on the site, and anyone can try a proposed rule in the simulator first.

Fees and the curve

The first 800M coins of every launch sell at one fixed price, $0.0001. The last 200M sell on a curve above the floor. The fee works the same way on both: 5% on every trade, split 1/2/2. On the curve, the 2% that goes to the reserve keeps lifting the floor underneath the price, so the gap between the price and the floor can shrink when a coin trades a lot.

The short version

  • 5% on every trade, split 1% creator, 2% launchpad, 2% reserve.
  • The reserve's 2% lifts the floor for every holder, and the floor is never lowered.
  • Creators earn from trading, not from a coin bag.
  • A round trip at the floor costs 9.75%, shown before you buy.
