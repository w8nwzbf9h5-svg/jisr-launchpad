Launch Beyond Limits.

A new token economy. Built on transparent reserves.

Landing Pad sells every coin at one fixed price and keeps the money in a reserve that no person controls. Any holder can sell back to that reserve at the floor price, at any time. Move the handle on the chart and watch the reserve keep up.

    Three proposed rule changes

    These are proposals, not the rules. Coins in the test keep today's rules. The rules for the real launch are settled after this vote. The vote is advisory: we read every answer, and the team decides.

    Take the ceiling off the price

    Today a coin's market stops selling when 190 million of its last 200 million coins are sold, at a market cap of about $40M. The proposal lets it sell almost all of them, so the price can keep rising with demand.

    The cost. The higher the price, the smaller the share of it that real money stands behind: about 1.2% at $40M, about 0.2% at $1B.

    A brake on mass selling

    When the price falls from its recent high, the fee on selling above the floor climbs gradually from 5% to 25%. The extra goes into the coin's floor reserve, not to the launchpad or the creator. Selling at the floor price stays at 5%.

    The cost. A seller in a falling market keeps less, and the fall is slowed, not stopped.

    A share of every premium for the floor

    10% of every dollar paid above the floor goes straight into the floor reserve and stays there, so the floor rises as the price does.

    The cost. Someone who buys above the floor and sells straight back gets about 81% back instead of 90%.

    How a coin lives

    The same four stages for every coin, on every network.

    1. LaunchThe creator pays the network fee and receives no coins. All one billion go to the coin's own market.
    2. Sale800 million coins at one fixed price. The value of every coin sold goes into the reserve.
    3. GraduationThe sale sells out. The reserve holds about $81,684 for those 800 million coins.
    4. After graduationThe last 200 million sell above the floor, and fees from trading keep raising the floor.

    What exists today

    A plain account, including what is not done.

    Networks
    Solana devnet and Ethereum Sepolia. Both are test networks.
    Solana program
    Deployed with its upgrade authority removed, so nobody can change it. The check above reads that from the network.
    Ethereum contracts
    47 automated contract tests and 26 browser test steps. Markets have no owner, no pause and no withdrawal.
    Fees
    5% of each trade: 1% to the coin's creator, 2% to the launchpad, 2% into the coin's own reserve. Paid out automatically as the coin trades.
    Independent audit
    Not done yet.
    Real money
    Not yet. Nothing is deployed on a real network.
