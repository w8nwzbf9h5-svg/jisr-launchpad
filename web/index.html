How it works

01One fixed price for everyone

Supply is 1 billion. 800 million are sold at one price, $0.0001 a coin, which is a $100K market cap from the first minute. The creator, the first buyer and the last buyer all pay the same.

02A reserve nobody can withdraw

The money that buys coins goes into the coin's own contract, in a stablecoin. The contract has no owner and no withdrawal function. It pays sellers by formula, the creator 1% and the launchpad 2%. Nothing else can leave.

03A floor covered 100%

Every coin that leaves the contract puts one floor price into the reserve, so the reserve can buy back every coin at the floor price at any time.

  • Buy with $1,000 and you hold $950 of coins after the buy fee.
  • Sell them back at the floor and you receive about $902.
  • That 9.75% is the most a sale buyer loses by selling at the floor.

04A floor that rises

2% of every trade stays in the reserve. Once the sale has sold out, that surplus lifts the floor price. It never comes down.

05After the sale sells out

The last 200 million coins trade above the floor. New demand pushes the price up. If buyers leave, the price falls back to the floor and stops there.

06Paying and getting paid

Pay with the stablecoin, or with the network's own currency, which is swapped to the stablecoin on the way in. When you sell, choose either one.

07What it cannot do

A buyer who pays more than the floor can lose the difference. A stablecoin can lose its peg or freeze an address. A bug in a contract can lose funds, and nobody can pause or patch a live market. The 2.5% limit is per address. This is a capped loss, never a guarantee.
